# Research confidence and open questions

## High-confidence conclusions

- The existing SfSkills corpus is valuable but inventory alone will not create sustained use.
- Salesforce and major vendors already provide broad raw tools, delivery automation, dependency/impact intelligence, or testing.
- Agentic hosts support increasingly composable skills, agents, MCP, commands, and hooks.
- Context overload and broad tool surfaces are recognized product problems, not theoretical concerns.
- Product Salesforce mutation is unnecessary for the first diagnostic/assurance wedge.
- Real behavioral QA requires known truth and actual selected context/evidence, not structural fixtures alone.

## Medium-confidence hypotheses

- A portable assurance layer can complement rather than compete directly with DevOps/org-intelligence incumbents.
- Visible evidence and independent review will materially improve practitioner trust and repeat use.
- The first three products are sufficient to prove the architecture across developer, release, admin, and security personas.
- A local-first product can expand into team/enterprise governance without losing transparency.

## Open questions to resolve empirically

- Which host/model combination delivers the best quality/cost/latency for each product?
- Do five or six focused subagents outperform fewer stages after latency and host limits?
- Which evidence normalization should be local wrapper versus official DX MCP direct use?
- How much project/org enrichment is needed before practitioners accept a diagnosis?
- Will users inspect evidence IDs or only value them when challenging a result?
- Which incumbent exports/APIs are practical to integrate?
- What percentage of real scenarios can be reproduced safely in scratch orgs?
- Which product creates the strongest repeat-use loop after P01/P02/P03?
- What licensing/distribution model supports a public standard, source-available corpus, and marketplace adapters?

The V2 implementation and design-partner program are explicitly structured to answer these questions rather than assuming them away.
