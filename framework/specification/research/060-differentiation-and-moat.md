# Differentiation and moat

## 1. Knowledge that has operational shape

The existing 1,034 packages are more defensible when treated as governed units with anti-patterns, examples, references, and selection metadata—not as a large prompt dump.

## 2. Evidence-native output

Most assistants produce prose with links. SfSkills should produce a typed claim/evidence graph, then render prose from it. That makes unsupported claims measurable and reviewable.

## 3. Context engineering as product infrastructure

SfSkills should know which instructions and evidence were loaded, why, at what cost, what was truncated, and what must survive compaction. This is more valuable than relying on a larger context window.

## 4. Salesforce-safe authority separation

Ordinary product agents remain read-only. Scratch-org setup authority is separate, guarded, and unavailable as a product tool. Upstream MCP annotations never substitute for hard policy.

## 5. Behavioral benchmark

A versioned set of known-truth Salesforce scenarios—run through the actual host, selected skills, evidence broker, and review path—can become a durable quality asset and community standard.

## 6. Cross-host portability

A user should be able to use the same product behavior and evidence model in Cursor, Claude Code, Copilot/VS Code, and Agentforce Vibes. Hosts compete on UX and models; SfSkills owns the Salesforce product contract.

## 7. Contribution flywheel

A production incident can yield:

- a new normalized evidence fixture;
- a regression scenario;
- a skill improvement;
- a context-selection rule;
- a reviewer rule;
- a product quality metric.

That turns usage into a governed improvement loop rather than an expanding prompt archive.
