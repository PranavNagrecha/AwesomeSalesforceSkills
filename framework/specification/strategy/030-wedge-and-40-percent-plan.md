# Wedge and 40% plan

## The honest target

“Beat 40% of the market” is not an acceptable public claim without an independently defined market, denominator, measurement period, and comparable products.

The initial 40% objective is:

> At least 40% of a deliberately recruited, qualified early-adopter cohort uses SfSkills for a second real Salesforce engineering job within fourteen days and remains weekly active after eight weeks.

This tests whether the product earns sustained use, not whether people star a repository.

## Qualified cohort

A participant qualifies when they:

- actively performs Salesforce development, administration, architecture, release, consulting, security, or Agentforce work;
- uses or is willing to use Cursor, Claude Code, Copilot, or Agentforce Vibes;
- has encountered at least one flagship job in the prior month;
- can run a fixture and, where allowed, a read-only org/project workflow;
- agrees to share sanitized product outcomes and friction.

The first cohort should be 25–50 practitioners across at least four personas and multiple org shapes. Report denominator and dropouts.

## Why the first three products

### Deployment failure triage

A high-frequency blocked-state job with structured job evidence, clear urgency, and measurable time-to-resolution.

### Apex test failure triage

Reuses the core while exercising stack traces, shared causes, source, automation, limits, user context, and context resilience.

### Access path explanation

Expands beyond developer-only workflows into admin/security/consulting use and tests layered org evidence where generic answers frequently overclaim.

## Adoption funnel

1. **Discovery:** specific problem-oriented pages and demos, not “1,034 skills.”
2. **Install:** local plugin/helper and doctor; no credentials required for the first fixture.
3. **First value:** one command, supplied fixture, useful result within fifteen minutes.
4. **Trust:** visible evidence IDs, target identity, limitations, independent review.
5. **Enrichment:** optional external project and explicit read-only org.
6. **Repeat:** second product/job within fourteen days.
7. **Team spread:** share a sanitized replay bundle or scenario.
8. **Contribution:** convert a real incident into a fixture/known-truth case.

## Initial thresholds

| Measure | Target |
|---|---:|
| Supported local install attempts that pass doctor | >=80% |
| Activated installs reaching first fixture result in 15 minutes | >=70% |
| Material claims with valid evidence references | >=98% |
| Accepted unsupported material claims in release scenario set | 0 |
| Prohibited Salesforce product mutations | 0 |
| Activated users with a second run within 14 days | >=40% |
| Qualified cohort weekly active after 8 weeks | >=40% |
| Retained users willing to recommend | >=50% |

## Technical “40% better” hypotheses

Measure, but do not promise until data exists:

- at least 40% reduction in median time to a useful first diagnosis versus raw evidence/vanilla host;
- at least 40% relative reduction in unsupported material claims;
- at least 40% relative increase in required-finding recall on hard Salesforce scenarios;
- at least 40% reduction in model-visible evidence bytes through normalization without reducing required-finding recall.

A claim must include scenario version, sample size, model/host versions, date, and scoring method.

## Design-partner cadence

- Week 0: install and fixture observation.
- Weeks 1–2: use P01/P02 on real existing results; capture time and acceptance.
- Weeks 3–4: add P03 and compare with normal workflow.
- Weeks 5–8: measure repeat, trust, team sharing, and retained use.
- Every week: classify friction into product, host, Salesforce access, knowledge, evidence, or UX; convert repeat defects into tests.
