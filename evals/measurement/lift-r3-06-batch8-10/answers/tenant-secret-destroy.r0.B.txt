**Not necessarily, but treat destruction as effectively permanent unless you exported the key first.**

**What the platform does**
- Setting a tenant secret to `Status = Destroyed` means it "can't encrypt or decrypt data," and files encrypted with it can no longer be downloaded (obj 264 L283782-283785).
- Data encrypted under that secret becomes inaccessible.

**When it is recoverable**
- If you previously exported the key, you can import it back into Salesforce and the related data becomes accessible again: "related data isn't accessible unless you previously exported the key and then import the key back" (L283928-283929).
- Cache-Only Key Service customers can move a cache-only secret from Destroyed back to Active (L283943-283944).

**When it is lost**
- You never exported the key, and the material (BYOK or otherwise) isn't held anywhere you can re-supply it. Salesforce-generated (`HSM`) secrets are the riskiest here, because you have nothing to re-import unless you exported first.
- In that case the ciphertext is unreadable, which is the point if you are doing deliberate crypto-shredding.

**Practical guidance**
1. Distinguish *Archived* from *Destroyed*. Rotation only archives the old secret. An archived secret can't encrypt new data but still decrypts old data (L283779-283781), so rotation is safe.
2. Before destroying, make sure the background encryption process has re-encrypted existing data under the new active key (api_meta 264 L127877-127882). Otherwise, anything still on the old secret goes dark. Check history duplicates and `FieldHistoryArchive` copies too, which have their own handling (secguide 262 L4899-4904).
3. Export the key and store it securely unless destroying the data is the explicit goal.
4. Rehearse in a sandbox first.

**Caveat:** I'm relying on the corpus lines above. I haven't confirmed any additional Help-only behaviours, such as whether Salesforce support can recover anything after destruction. Don't assume it can.
