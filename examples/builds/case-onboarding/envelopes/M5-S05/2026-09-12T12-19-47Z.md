## 8. Process Observations

**Healthy**
- The step's `acceptance_tests[0]` description predicted the checker's exact output before the
  artefact existed — score 95, one `SharingRules` WARN, 0 blocking, exit 0 — and the run matched it
  line for line. A test description that names the expected non-zero-severity finding is what
  stopped a correct artefact from reading as a failure. (`plan.json steps[M5-S05].acceptance_tests[0]`)
- Every fact the manifest needed was already written down somewhere in the build: the version
  decision in the `milestone:M3` gate note, the Apex exception in the contract, the blocked reasons
  on the two blocked steps, the ordering hazards collated by M5-S04. This step was a join across
  existing records rather than a new decision, which is the shape a build's last step should have.

**Concerning**
- The tester's always-on `manifest` check reads the step's **own** artefact directory in both
  directions, and this step's directory holds only `package.xml` and `deploy-order.md` while its 56
  members' files live under sixteen sibling step directories. Read literally, every member is a
  member-without-a-file. The step's `acceptance_tests[2]` describes the whole-tree reading the check
  actually needs; nothing in `agents/step-tester/AGENT.md` Step 3 carves out the build-level
  manifest the way rule 1 carves out the `apex-builder` step. Worth a contract line before the next
  build hits it.
- This run claimed the step `running` **after** the owning agent's reads and writes rather than
  before them, inverting Step 3's ordering. Nothing was lost — the step was `pending`, in `next` and
  un-gated throughout, and the claim was written before the terminal transition — but a crash
  between the first write and the claim would have left artefacts on disk under a step still reading
  `pending`. Recorded rather than glossed.

**Ambiguous**
- `set-status … running` printed three unrelated WARNs about non-standard checker argument forms on
  M5-S02 and M5-S04 before doing its own work. They are the documented library backlog in
  `standards/build-orchestration.md` § 5, not about this step, but they arrive on every plan write
  and read as this step's problem.
- M5 now has one step left un-run (`M5-S02`, blocked) and one gate pending. This step's three
  `manual` acceptance lines (W12 1–3 of 3) cannot be ticked by any agent, and two of them assert
  properties of the two files just written, so the gate reader has to open both files rather than
  read a verdict.
