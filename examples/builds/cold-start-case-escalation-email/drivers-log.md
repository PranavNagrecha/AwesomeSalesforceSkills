## Five things that most helped
1. `examples/builds/opp-amount-lock/` (and its precise worked example in `standards/build-orchestration.md` § 3.1) — a complete, structurally correct prior build at a comparable scale. Reading its `plan.json`, its verification.json, and its envelopes let me copy a correct shape instead of reverse-engineering the JSON Schema from scratch at every stage.
2. `python3 scripts/build_plan.py --help` and each subcommand's own `--help`/AGENT.md Plan section — the CLI enforces its own contract (refuses malformed writes, tells you the exact next command), which made it very hard to silently go off-script.
3. Actually running things instead of describing them: `--help` on every checker before citing it, a throwaway fixture to prove `check_email_templates.py` really does fail on a Flow-only artefact, a negative fixture (stripped fault connectors) to prove the flow checker can actually fail, and `check-outputs`/`validate_envelope.py` after every write. This caught two real, substantive defects (a missing citation for the `emailSimple` action shape, and an inapplicable acceptance test) that a "plausible-sounding" pass would have missed entirely.
4. `standards/decision-trees/automation-selection.md` and `flow-pattern-selector.md` — deterministic, quotable decision trees turned "should this be a Flow?" from a judgment call into a cited, verifiable answer (with verbatim `branch_quote`s the verifier could check word-for-word against the tree file).
5. The § 3.1 sizing rule's explicit, printed formula (D/S/O/X counts) — it forced a concrete tier decision (`feature`, because the requirement names two objects) rather than letting me eyeball the ceremony level from the automation's apparent simplicity, which is exactly the trap the worked "ask"-tier validation-rule example would have led me into by surface analogy.

## Five things that most hindered
1. My own Stage-2 scoping judgment: I excluded `admin/flow-for-admins` from the clarification harvest (bucketing it with the escalation/routing skills I was deliberately keeping out of scope), when it actually holds the one deployable example (`emailSimple` actionCalls) the whole step depends on. This was only caught because Stage 4 (verification) independently re-derived what mechanism the step needed and went looking for it — a pure "did the harvested skills get read" check would not have caught it.
2. `admin/email-templates-and-alerts`'s checker (`check_email_templates.py`) is scoped to classic `EmailTemplate`/`.email-meta.xml` artefacts only, but nothing in the skill's frontmatter/description signals that its checker has a narrower reach than its own prose guidance — the SKILL.md content (sender identity, merge context) reads as generally applicable to "email from Salesforce," so citing its checker for a Flow-embedded email was a natural, wrong inference that only running the checker against a real fixture exposed.
3. Two small self-authored XML/JSON slips (an unclosed `<connector>` tag in the Flow XML; a missing `suggested_followup_agent`/`followup_reason` pair in a process observation) — neither was a process gap, both were caught immediately by tooling (an IDE diagnostic hook, `validate_envelope.py`), but each cost a re-edit-and-recheck cycle.
4. The § 3.1 contract's own text is occasionally ambiguous about *where* a skipped ceremony's content should land at `feature` scale specifically (the workbook-optional rule has an explicit RUN.md redirect at `ask` scale but no stated alternative at `feature` scale) — I had to make and document a judgment call (skip the workbook, rely on traceability.md + decisions.md) rather than follow an explicit instruction.
5. The sheer number of required artifacts per stage (envelope JSON + markdown twin + validate_envelope.py, for every one of 6 stages) is a lot of mechanical ceremony for a one-step build — appropriate per the contract's own guarantees, but it meant most of the wall-clock time went to schema-shaped bookkeeping rather than to the Salesforce design judgment itself.
