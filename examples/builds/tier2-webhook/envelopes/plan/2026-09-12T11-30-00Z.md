## 6. The two answers the library contradicted

Both are recorded as decisions with assumptions behind them rather than planned around.

**Q23 — the Queueable's running user.** The answer asserts the Automated Process entity and asks the
plan to settle how that entity is granted the External Credential principal.
`skills/apex/apex-user-and-permission-checks` states async Apex runs as the async context user, with
scheduled jobs and platform-event triggers as the two exceptions — a trigger-enqueued Queueable is
neither — and `skills/flow/flow-and-platform-events` Gotcha 10 records that Automated Process holds
no profile and no permission set assignments at all. So the grant as described cannot be made.
Decision **D14** settles it: the permission set goes to everyone who can escalate. Assumption **A2**
carries the library's own hedge (the word "often") and the instruction to confirm in a scratch org.
If D14 is wrong, every escalation returns 401 and every escalation lands in the failure record — a
loud failure rather than a silent one, which is why it is planned this way and gated at `M1-S02`.

**Q9/Q10 — publish sequencing.** Q9's proposed default was to publish first, then call out. Inside
one Queueable `execute()` that is not buildable: a publish-after-commit publish is a DML statement
and a callout cannot follow DML in the same transaction. Decision **D10** moves the publish into the
escalating transaction's service layer, which satisfies Q10's rollback requirement directly through
`publishBehavior` and preserves Q9's intent that the dashboard see the escalation regardless of
webhook outcome.

## 10. Process observations

**Healthy.** All nine declared checkers take `--manifest-dir`, so no step needed a non-standard
argument form. Eleven of fourteen decisions landed on a tree branch that says what the decision says.
Mandating `templates/apex/HttpClient.cls` is what keeps the per-Case callout loop out of
`check_callouts_and_http_integrations.py` rule 4 — the canonical template earns its mandate rather
than being a style preference.

**Concerning.** (high) The Q23 / Automated Process conflict above. (medium) § 4's `access` row and
`metadata-builder`'s own Step 2 table both stop at "PermissionSet / PSG / sharing XML", so the layer
documents no home for `NamedCredential` and `ExternalCredential`; `M1-S02`'s notes reason it onto
`access` from `metadata-builder` Step 3's rule that the step's `skills[]` is authoritative.
(medium) The library contradicts itself on hourly schedule-triggered Flows —
`flow/flow-time-based-patterns` lists hourly, `flow/scheduled-flows` says the granularity may not
fit. D11 routes away from Flow rather than resolving it. (low) `M1-S03` carries ten classes and
triggers across two sObjects because the five-step ceiling leaves no room for a sixth step and
`apex-builder` refuses more than one feature per invocation, so splitting is not free either.

**Ambiguous.** The `AuthHeader` merge-field grammar (A5). The fit-gap scope multiplier's
"more than 1 user persona class", which reads either as roles or as persona classes and moves two
rows between L and XL; the internal-only reading is taken and named in the rows' notes. And `M1-S05`,
which exists because of a build-layer contract clause rather than a business requirement, so its
fit-gap row states a delivery requirement the requester never phrased — the alternative was a step no
`fit_gap` row reaches.

**Suggested follow-ups.** `plan-verifier` next, always — and its grounding lens should re-derive D10
and D14 first. `waf-assessor` after the plan gate, because four `adr_required` decisions is more
unrecorded architecture than a five-step feature usually leaves behind.
