## What the playbook/CLI did not support

- **The task's literal request to add `TestUserFactory` to "the step's `package.xml`"
  was not carried out.** This `apex-builder`-owned `automation` step has never declared
  a `package.xml` of its own. `standards/build-orchestration.md` § 5 **The Apex
  exception** puts every `ApexClass`/`ApexTrigger` member this step produces into the
  build-level manifest step **M1-S05** instead (`depends_on` this step), and this
  step's own `manifest` acceptance test already records skipped-not-applicable, naming
  M1-S05. Writing a local `package.xml` here would contradict that recorded exception
  and violate § 4 condition 2 (an agent may not declare an output its own Output
  Contract does not produce — `apex-builder`'s names none). `TestUserFactory` is
  recorded as a thirteenth member M1-S05 needs to add (deploy-order.md § 0b, § 2); doing
  so is out of scope for an M1-S03-only re-run.
- **`amend-step` could not be used** to add `TestUserFactory.cls`/`.cls-meta.xml` to
  this step's declared `outputs[]`, because `amend-step` is refused once a step has left
  `pending` (this step is `running`). The two new files join `TestDataFactory` and
  `MockHttpResponseGenerator` as undeclared-but-shipped artefacts — `check-outputs`
  still passes because it only confirms declared paths.
- **`check_test_class_standards.py` is not in this step's declared `acceptance_tests`**,
  so running it and reporting its result is outside the normal "run the step's own
  checkers exactly as declared" loop; it was run because the task named it explicitly,
  and its absence from the declared set is recorded as a process observation
  (tooling-gap) rather than silently left for a future run to rediscover.
